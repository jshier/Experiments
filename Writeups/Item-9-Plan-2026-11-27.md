## 9b. Short-distance back-references (first: it's on the decoders' hot path)

**Now** (`bytesapi-after`): with 3–32-byte matches, distances 2–7 take 35–37 µs and 312–383 K instructions; distance 8
takes 18 µs and 130 K. The note blamed store-to-load forwarding; the instructions say most of it is the path:
`copy(fromDistance:count:allowingOverrun:)` sends any distance under 8 to the exact `copy(fromDistance:count:)`, whose
`_copyBack` calls the out-of-line `_copyRepeating` (a variable-width `Load.block`, then `_fill`'s loop). Distance 1
is a `memset`. Users: DEFLATE's and Brotli's decoders (overrun allowed).

**Plan:** in the overrun path, distances 1–7 inline: load the 8 bytes ending at the destination's start (one
unaligned load; its first `8 − d` bytes are older output, so it needs `destination ≥ 8`, else the exact path), build a
16-byte pattern with one `tbl` from a per-distance index vector (`i % d`, a 7-entry table of `SIMD16<UInt8>` constants
built as literals), store it at the destination, then for the rest copy forward with stride `16 − 16 % d` (a multiple of
`d` at most 16), so each later 16-byte store reads bytes already final (or rotate the pattern by `tbl` again). Up to 32
bytes, two stores with no branch on length; longer, a loop. Measure the back-reference rows and DEFLATE/Brotli decode
rows on binary corpora (samba, x-ray, mozilla) and text (dickens, which barely uses short distances: no regression
allowed); check the decode loop's length (`Tools/loops`) since an inline path grew it 15% once (the reason
`_copyRepeating` went out of line).

## 9c. Base64's decoder on the M1 Max

**Now:** 1 MB decodes at 2.35 GB/s (427 µs), 4.5x Foundation. The kernel was ranked on the M3, where a 4-register
`tbl` is cheaper; on the M1 Max it's three vector slots and a 4-register `tbx` four (8-cycle latency).

**Plan:** count the kernel's vector µops per 64-byte block with the M1 Max's costs (`Tools/loops`, `Tools/mca` as a lower
bound), and try the classic 2-register-lookup decode (high nibble / low nibble `tbl`s, as in Muła and Lemire's
"Faster Base64 decoding with AVX2/NEON" adapted) as a variant in one binary (`@inline(never)` functions over a shared
body, A/B alternated). Keep whichever is faster on the M1 Max without being slower on the 40–95-byte rows; the M3's
numbers can't be re-measured here, so a change that's a wash on the M1 Max stays out.

## 9a. CRC-32 with scalar `crc32x` streams beside the `pmull` fold (last: least practical value)

**Now:** the fold runs about 61 GB/s in cache, 90% of its 6-cycle-per-128-byte vector bound; from memory it's at the
load rate already (about 60). Scalar `crc32x` (1 a cycle, 3 cycles of latency) on three streams could add about 8 bytes
a cycle in cache, so up to about +35% there and nothing from memory. In every client CRC-32 runs beside a decoder 50
times slower (inflate) or the file system, so the gain is a fraction of a percent of any real workload.

**Plan:** a probe only: the hybrid in fixed macro-blocks (the vector part and three scalar streams each a fixed length,
combined with precomputed shift constants, so no run-time `x^n mod P`), measured at 4 KB, 64 KB, 256 KB, 4 MB. Kept only
if it gains at least 20% in cache and nothing is slower at any size; otherwise recorded.
